Subtitle Studio takes a file, listens to it, and gives you captions you can edit,
style and burn in. It works on any video or audio file — it does not have to be
something you made in Diwche.

## Do it

1. Open **Subtitles** in the left rail, under Library.
2. Under **Video / audio file**, choose your file.
3. Set the Whisper options below it (next section).
4. Press **Transcribe** and wait. A panel shows what it is doing.

When it finishes you land in the two-column studio: the video with live captions
on the left, the controls on the right.

## The options that matter

<Shot name="subs-whisper" label="Whisper Options" />

| Option | What to set |
| --- | --- |
| **Task** | *Transcribe* keeps the spoken language. *Translate to English* gives you English captions over any language |
| **Language** | Leave on **Auto-detect** unless it guesses wrong, then name it |
| **Vocabulary hint** | Names, places and jargon it will otherwise mangle. Worth filling in |
| **Voice activity filter** | Leave on. Skips silence |
| **Word-level timestamps** | Leave on. Required for karaoke and word-by-word styles |

The **Vocabulary hint** is the one people skip and then re-do the whole
transcript by hand. If your video says *Rijksmuseum*, *ultramarine* and
*Vermeer*, type those three words in.

## A note on the backend

A chip near the top reads **● Mac**, **● NAS** or **○ Unavailable** — which
machine is doing the listening. If it says CPU, it will be slower and less
accurate. If it says transcription is turned off, an administrator has to switch
it on.
