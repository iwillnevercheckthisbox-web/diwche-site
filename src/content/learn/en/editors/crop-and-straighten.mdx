**Crop** is the first tool in the rail, and the editor opens on it.

## Crop it

1. Pick a shape from the row of ratios, or leave it on **Freeform**.
2. Drag the frame on the picture to choose what is kept, and **Photo zoom** to
   size the picture inside it.
3. **Done**, or **Cancel** to abandon it. **↺ Revert** puts the picture back.

## The shapes

| Ratio | What for |
| --- | --- |
| **4:5** | A feed post. The tallest Instagram allows, so the biggest on a phone |
| **1:1** | Square |
| **9:16** | Story and Reel |
| **16:9** | Landscape |
| **Freeform** | Any shape you drag |

**4:5 is the right default for a feed post.** A square wastes a fifth of the
screen a scrolling reader gives you. A **⌾ Portrait** button flips the current
ratio on its side.

## Straighten and flip

**Straighten** is a dial in tenths of a degree. Levelling a horizon is usually
the difference between a photo that looks taken and one that looks snapped.

**↻ Rotate** turns it in quarter turns. **⇔ Flip H** and **⇕ Flip V** mirror it.

## The safe-area overlay

On story and reel sizes an overlay marks the parts of the screen Instagram's own
interface covers: your handle at the top, and the reply box, caption and buttons
at the bottom.

**Keep faces and words out of those bands.** It is the single most common reason
a design that looked right in the editor looks broken in the app.
