Trimming is dragging the end of a clip. The only thing you have to decide first
is what happens to everything after it.

## The two tools

<Shot name="ve-toolbar" callouts={[
  { x: 9, y: 50, text: 'Edges stop at the neighbour' },
  { x: 22, y: 50, text: 'Edges push everything along' },
]} />

**↖ Select** (key **A**) — dragging an edge stops at the next clip. Shorten a
clip and a **gap** opens where it was. Nothing else moves.

**⇥ Trim** (key **T**) — dragging an edge moves everything after it. Shorten a
clip and the rest of the video slides up to meet it. This is a *ripple*.

Use Select when you are fine-tuning one shot and want the timing of everything
else left alone. Use Trim when you are tightening the whole thing.

## Trim it

1. Hover the left or right end of a clip. A handle appears.
2. Drag it inward to shorten, outward to lengthen — as far as the original
   footage goes, and no further. The handle stops and marks itself when you hit
   that limit.
3. Let go.

## Cut a clip in two

Put the playhead where you want the cut and press **S**, or select the clip and
press **✂ Split** in the Inspector. You now have two clips and a new cut between
them.

## Remove a clip

- **Delete** takes the clip out and leaves a gap.
- **Shift + Delete** takes it out and closes the gap.
- **⇤ Close gaps** under the timeline removes every gap on the selected clip's
  track at once.

## Snapping

Edges snap to the playhead, to other clips, to markers and to your In and Out
points, and a vertical line shows what they caught on. **Hold Alt** (Option on a
Mac) while dragging to ignore snapping entirely.

## Ripple, globally

**⇄ Ripple on** in the top bar is a separate switch. With it on, anything that
makes Video 1 longer or shorter — a Trim-tool drag, an insert, a ripple delete —
moves the other tracks with it, so your music and titles keep their place
against the picture. Turn it off when you want the other tracks to stay put.
