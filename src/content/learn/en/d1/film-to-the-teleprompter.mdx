The script scrolls over your own camera preview. You read; it records.

## Set it up first

<Shot name="d1-idea" label="Before you record" />

Three controls above the stage:

- **Speed** — how fast the words scroll. Start slower than feels right; you can
  always do another take.
- **Text size** — bigger if you are further from the screen.
- **Mirror my view** — flips *your preview only*, so it feels like a mirror. The
  recording is not flipped.

And two pickers:

- **Camera** — *Default camera*, or another one.
- **Microphone** — *Default microphone*, or another one.

If the pickers show no names yet, the browser has not been given permission. Let
it use the camera once and the names appear.

## Record

1. **● Start recording.** It reads *Turning the camera on…* while the camera
   wakes.
2. Read. A red badge shows the elapsed time, and a wave moves while it hears you.
3. **■ Done**.

## After a take

- **↺ Record again** — throws it away and starts over. Do this freely.
- **Make it →** — sends the take off to be cut.

It tells you what happens next:

> We'll cut the pauses and the stumbles, caption it, add the opening line and
> your sign-off, and tell you when it's ready to look at. You can close this
> page.

That is worth trusting. **You do not have to be perfect.** Pauses, restarts and
filler words are taken out — and the review screen lists exactly what was
removed, so you can put anything back.

## Practical advice

- Look at the **camera**, not at the words. Set the text size big enough that you
  can read it in your peripheral vision.
- One long take beats five careful short ones. The cutting is done for you.
- If you fluff a line, stop, pause for a second, and say it again. The restart is
  detected and the first attempt is dropped.
